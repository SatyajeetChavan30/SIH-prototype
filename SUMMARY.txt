# JalRaksha: Dam-Break Inundation Modelling System
## Smart India Hackathon 2026 (Problem Statement 26161, NTRO-Sponsored)

---

## What is JalRaksha?

JalRaksha is a Python-based automated system for dam-break and natural river-blockage inundation modelling. It combines physics-based numerical solvers with satellite validation to generate rapid flood-risk screening assessments for disaster management authorities.

**Use Case**: Tier-1 rapid screening tool for CWC (Central Water Commission), NDMA, and state disaster management agencies to prioritize dams for detailed expert studies and support evacuation planning.

---

## Core Technology

### Dual-Solver Architecture
- **Near-field (3D SPH)**: Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics for violent breach jet (hundreds of metres, tens of seconds) — non-hydrostatic, Lagrangian
- **Far-field (2D SWE)**: Delft3D-class depth-averaged shallow-water equation solver for basin propagation (tens of km, hours) — Eulerian finite volume, HLLC + Audusse
- **Comparison**: Quantitative metrics (CSI, F1, RMSE) between the two models

### Data Sources (All Open, No Login)
- **Copernicus DEM GLO-30** — 30m resolution elevation (public AWS COGs)
- **Google Earth Engine** — Sentinel-1 SAR (observed flood extent validation), GHSL (population), Open Buildings (CC BY 4.0)
- **CWC NRLD/NRSD** — National dam registers (public)
- **ESA WorldCover** — Land-use classification (open)

### Numerical Method
- **Flux scheme**: HLLC Riemann solver with transverse-momentum correction
- **Reconstruction**: MUSCL with Audusse hydrostatic method (well-balanced)
- **Wet/dry treatment**: Depth-positivity limiter, adaptive CFL
- **Friction**: Manning's n parameterization
- **Compiler**: Numba JIT (@njit, CUDA-ready)

## Input & Output

### Input
```
Dam location: lat/lon (metric CRS, no degrees)
Dam height: metres
Gross storage: million cubic metres
Breach mode: overtopping | piping | seepage
```

### Output
- **Raster maps**: Depth, velocity, arrival-time (GeoTIFF, Cloud-Optimized)
- **Vector layers**: Inundation extent polygon (Shapefile, KML/KMZ)
- **Exposure analysis**: Buildings, roads, population at risk
- **Web dashboard**: Interactive maps, time-series plots, comparison metrics
- **Caveats**: Model agreement scores, uncertainty bands printed on map

**Validation**: Cross-checked against Chamoli 2021 real event (published <5% travel-time benchmark from Shugar et al., Science)

## Key Constraints & Rules

**Hard Rules** (Non-negotiable):
- ✅ No India-WRIS, Bhuvan, CartoDEM (all geo-fenced/broken/login-gated)
- ✅ Never claim to be Delft3D (say "Delft3D-class")
- ✅ Every empirical coefficient flagged and sourced from primary literature
- ✅ Everything runs fully offline from cache (network-independent demo)
- ✅ Report inundation envelopes and arrival times (not point depths — 30m DEM too coarse)
- ✅ Metric CRS only (EPSG:32643 for India or equivalent UTM)
- ✅ License-clean outputs (Copernicus + Google Open Buildings; avoid CC BY-NC-SA)

**Tier-1 Positioning**:
- Rapid screening tool (minutes per site)
- No bathymetric survey required
- Works on transboundary and inaccessible terrain
- Complementary to CWC's Tier-2/3 detailed studies, not a replacement

## Testing & Validation Strategy

**Analytical Tests** (Exact Solutions):
- Ritter dry-bed dam-break (1D)
- Stoker wet-bed dam-break (1D)
- Thacker parabolic bowl (2D oscillation)

**Blocking Correctness Gates** (Phase 1):
- Lake-at-rest: <0.1% velocity over any bathymetry
- Mass conservation: <0.1% volume loss over 1,000 timesteps
- Dry-bed robustness: no NaNs, negative depths, or division errors

**Real-World Benchmarks**:
- Malpasset (1959) — published arrival-time field measurements
- Chamoli 2021 (India) — only Indian dam-break with pre/post 2m DEMs (published <5% travel-time benchmark)

**CI Gates**: Lake-at-rest and mass-conservation tests must pass before PR merge.

## Code Organization

```
jalraksha/
├── config.py          — Config loading & validation (Phase 0)
├── cli.py             — CLI entry point (Phase 0)
├── cache.py           — Offline-first cache management (Phase 0)
├── dem.py             — DEM fetch from Copernicus (Phase 0)
├── solver/            — 2D SWE core (Phase 1+)
├── terrain/           — DEM conditioning, breach (Phase 2–3)
├── export/            — GeoTIFF, Shapefile, KML (Phase 5)
└── sph/               — SPH near-field coupling (Phase 7)

tests/                 — Unit tests (co-located with modules)
tools/sih-presentation/  — SIH deck generation (isolated)
```

**Architecture Principles**:
- Deep modules (high functionality, simple interface)
- No circular dependencies (Phase 0 → 1 → ... → 7)
- Test co-location (import modules directly, not through CLI)
- Separation of concerns (solver/export/presentation isolated)

## Why JalRaksha Matters

**Social Impact**: Arrival-time maps enable evacuation lead-time. Phuktal 2015: 3,000 moved, zero deaths (vs Chamoli 2021: 204 died, no warning).

**Economic**: Zero licence cost (all open-source); automation replaces weeks of expert setup per site.

**Strategic**: No-survey capability for transboundary and inaccessible terrain; critical for Himalayan and trans-border river valleys.

**Scientific**: SPH vs 2D SWE agreement metrics are a publishable contribution, not just a repackaged UI.
